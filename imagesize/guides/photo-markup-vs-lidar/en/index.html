PHOTO DIMENSION GUIDES

Photo dimension markup or LiDAR measurement?

Two apps can show similar dimension arrows while doing very different jobs. Before choosing one, ask where the number comes from. This is more useful than comparing how many tools appear in the toolbar.

When you already know the length

Photo markup places a number you provide beside an edge or an arrow. It is useful when you have measured with a tape, received an approved dimension or want to explain a detail. The photograph supplies context; the measurement comes from you.

When a photo includes a known reference

Some tools scale a photograph from a known length. The reference and target need compatible geometry; perspective and different depths can invalidate a simple scale. An arbitrary photo without a known scale or capture information does not determine an absolute physical length.

When the capture includes depth

SnapDim uses a new photo captured on a LiDAR-capable iPhone together with depth and camera information. You can select an object boundary, add two-point dimensions and refine the annotations. Measurements need usable depth and endpoints on the same surface; reflective or transparent materials and occlusion can interfere.

Choose by your starting point

Known dimensions or old photos: look for manual entry or reference-calibration support. A physical planar object in front of you: a depth-supported capture may fit the task. Critical fitting or fabrication: verify the result with an appropriate physical measuring tool.

What SnapDim includes

SnapDim provides new LiDAR capture, object-boundary selection, two-point dimensions, refinement, local records and annotated-image sharing. It requires a LiDAR-capable iPhone with iOS 17 or later. This version does not provide camera-roll calibration, floor-plan scanning or CAD export.
